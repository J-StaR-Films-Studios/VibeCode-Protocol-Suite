# Media production and review

## Source meaning

Use transcripts to locate candidates, then verify the source passage and context. Check names, quotations and claims against the recording or another reliable source. Preserve complete enough thoughts and the speaker's meaning. Avoid beginnings mid-word, abrupt endings and cuts that reverse a qualification.

Preserve the original voice unless another treatment is requested. Subtitles are a creative choice, not a default requirement. When wanted, transcribe and time them against the actual audio.

For source framing failures, consider another passage or a shorter complete thought. Distinguish source defects from edit defects. Do not fabricate missing footage or audio to conceal a problem.

## Composition

Use actual frames to choose a crop. A static centre crop may lose a moving speaker. Sample the entire excerpt, especially turns, gestures and camera pans.

If tracking is needed, follow the intended subject rather than whichever face a detector finds. Audience members, posters and background faces can mislead automatic crops. Inspect the resulting sequence and avoid unnecessary movement.

Judge headline, brand and subject together. Protect faces and gestures. Keep essential text away from platform controls and verify current safe areas when relevant. Use unused space only when it improves the composition.

Measure rendered text. Font metrics, wrapping and spacing can push a second line into the video despite a seemingly safe font size. Inspect every headline at final dimensions; different words break the same template differently.

## Photographs and generated assets

Preserve original photographs. Keep grading credible and consistent, attending to skin tones, exposure and source quality. Choose collage crops that retain people and purpose. Avoid near-identical photographs across adjacent posts.

Use real photographs for claims about actual people, places and events. Generated graphics can support a design, but do not present synthetic scenes as documentary evidence. Use the required image workflow for generation or edits. A mockup is not a finished photographic asset.

## Export evidence

Review in proportion to the edit:

- Check dimensions, orientation, duration, compatibility and the expected audio stream.
- Decode the full export to catch corruption or truncation.
- Inspect beginning, middle, end and enough intermediate frames to expose motion or crop failures. Dense contact sheets help with short excerpts.
- Play and listen when timing, intelligibility, cuts or synchronization need confirmation.
- Check the uploaded preview when platform crops, thumbnails or compression may alter the result.

Keep these claims separate. Decoding does not prove composition. Contact sheets do not prove continuous playback or synchronization. Audio-level measurements do not prove a sentence ends naturally.

Fix confirmed defects in the affected version and rerun relevant checks. Preserve unrelated outputs. Retire or clearly label stale review images so they cannot be mistaken for the final version.
