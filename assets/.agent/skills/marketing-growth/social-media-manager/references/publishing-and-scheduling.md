# Publishing and scheduling

## Before a mutation

Prefer a supported connector for covered actions. Otherwise follow the active computer-use skill and browser documentation. Confirm the destination using account identity, page ID or another authoritative signal. A familiar title can be ambiguous when several accounts are open.

Use fresh UI state after actions and build interactions from observed controls. Reuse the selected browser. Follow documented recovery when a tab fails. A transient timeout does not prove an upload failed.

Respect authorization for routine uploads, posts and scheduling. Keep required approvals attached to the specific consequential action. Read-only inspection and reversible preparation can continue while a separate action awaits approval.

## Upload and save

Select the exact approved file. Wait for evidence that upload and processing have reached the state needed for the next step. Next can be enabled before upload finishes. Save may appear early without completing a save.

Keep draft-save, schedule and publish actions distinct. Do not click Publish to finish an upload or unblock a disabled control. Do not add boosts, ads, collaboration or access changes incidentally.

After submitting, inspect the result proving the requested state. A generic shared alert may accompany a draft save. The saved Drafts or Scheduled entry is stronger evidence. Verify date and timezone after scheduling.

For asynchronous processing, reconcile the existing operation before retrying. A temporarily empty filtered list may reflect propagation delay. Refresh or inspect the known post before submitting another copy. Record an unresolved save when a bounded investigation cannot establish its state.

## Replace in place

Before deleting a rejected post, inspect its editor for media replacement. Drafts and scheduled posts may support replacing video while preserving captions and dates. Reopen the saved entry to verify persistence.

Removing an attachment in an unsaved editor differs from permanently deleting a saved post. Evaluate the actual action. If permanent deletion is unavoidable, explain the exact post and consequence and obtain the required action-time confirmation. Continue independent preparation where possible.

Do not assume a scheduling dialog can move a post to Drafts. Inspect its available controls instead of guessing a recovery route.

## Rolling date limits

Inspect the account's live date picker or documented limit. Limits can vary by account, format, tool and time. Do not hard-code a remembered maximum.

Keep the calendar in the user's timezone and confirm the platform's interpretation. Browser notification times and execution-host timezones do not establish the intended publication time.

When a date is unavailable, retain the reviewed draft and intended timestamp. Do not compress the cadence or publish the batch immediately to make the task appear finished.

For authorized follow-up, update an existing recurring task rather than duplicating it. Include the authoritative record, destination, eligibility, current media selection and reconciliation procedure. Require live checks before submissions and verified results before status updates. Keep unchanged runs quiet.

## Observed Facebook behaviour

The following occurred in a business-dashboard workflow in September 2026. Confirm the current UI before relying on them:

- Draft and scheduled editors allowed replacing media in place. Scheduled dates survived saving.
- Reel creation advanced while media uploaded. Early Save attempts sometimes left the editor open without saving.
- Draft saves produced generic sharing alerts and processing notifications. Drafts established the actual state.
- Entries briefly disappeared from filtered lists during processing, then reappeared after refresh.
- The date picker enforced a rolling limit, requiring distant posts to remain drafts.
- Lists loaded more rows while scrolling. Their initial page did not represent the whole queue.

These observations guide inspection. They are not permanent platform rules or stable selectors.
