# Asset sourcing

## Choose before searching

Define the intended shot and its job first. "Find something about content" is not a useful sourcing brief. "A populated social profile scrolling through varied product posts, readable in a vertical crop" is.

Inventory the project's existing media before acquiring more. Inspect the candidate's actual frames and relevant moving segment. Filenames, thumbnails and search snippets do not prove suitability. Prefer authorized real examples when the passage describes the client's work. Use licensed stock, generated illustrative footage or authored graphics when those better serve the idea and the brief permits them.

## Acquire assets or recommend within scope

Distinguish a sourcing recommendation from an authorized edit. When the user asks you to build and permits acquiring online B-roll or images, carry out the sourcing: search public sources, inspect actual candidates and useful time ranges, verify the exact asset's licence, download permitted material into the project and use it in the rough cut. Do not stop at search terms or treat the initial absence of B-roll as a reason to animate every line. Honour host permissions and account/access restrictions. Purchases, paid generation and private-media uploads still need authorization.

Keep acquisition proportional to the chosen treatment. Reject attractive footage that does not demonstrate the intended action or fit the crop. Do not add stock solely to meet a B-roll quota, and do not present illustrative stock as the client's actual work. Preserve source URLs, creator credits, licence evidence and original-file mappings. Inspect downloaded files, not just their listing pages.

If an access restriction, uncertain licence or missing real-client evidence blocks the chosen route, identify that specific gap and give a usable alternative. Do not quietly manufacture portfolio proof. When the request is plan-only, execution is unavailable or acquisition is not authorized, provide a recommendation rather than downloading or generating assets.

For each missing asset that needs a recommendation, provide:

1. The selected visual and why it belongs on this line.
2. An actual reference link and the useful detail you inspected, if accessible.
3. A few targeted search phrases for the chosen source, or a copyable image/video prompt.
4. The proposed crop, action, approximate hold and connection to adjacent shots.
5. Any acquisition, permission or licence constraint that affects use.

Give one recommended direction. Add an alternative only when the first route is unavailable or a genuine creative choice remains. Do not offload a long list of unrelated options onto the user.

A reference is not necessarily a usable asset. Identify portfolio work as inspiration unless reuse permission is confirmed. Do not present search results as inspected footage or imply a paid template has been licensed. Record provenance and licence for downloaded assets. Keep original filenames or a clear mapping to the originals.

## Write useful search terms

Search for visible subjects and actions, then narrow by framing or format. Examples:

- `social media profile scrolling screen recording`
- `macro wristwatch moving second hand`
- `camera filming skincare product behind the scenes`
- `ecommerce static ad creative portfolio`

Searches for finished designs usually need image or template sources rather than generic video libraries. A finished still can be animated in the editor. Searches for a process need the process visibly occurring, not only an adjacent object such as a person at a laptop.

## Write useful generation prompts

State subject, action, shot/framing, composition, intended duration or still format, visual treatment and constraints. Separate image generation from motion instructions. Avoid exact timing, typography or UI requirements a generator cannot reliably satisfy; author those elements in the editor when precision matters.

For an illustrative static ad:

> Create a finished 4:5 ecommerce image advertisement for an unbranded skincare bottle. Realistic product photography, a deliberate graphic layout and a short headline reading "PRODUCT SPOTLIGHT." Use an off-white background, charcoal type and restrained yellow accents for this example. Show the flat, front-facing finished artwork, not a phone mockup, blank template, desk scene or design interface. No discounts, testimonials, medical claims or performance statistics.

The colours above illustrate one approved project, not a required palette. Check generated text before using it. Generate clean imagery and add typography separately if necessary.

For a feed insert, ask for varied completed posts, a stable interface and actual scrolling. Avoid invented follower or engagement figures and real usernames. When the interface must stay exact, build the feed from approved stills and animate the scroll instead of relying on generated UI.

A prompt is a deliverable, not permission to submit it. Obtain approval for paid generation, purchases and uploads to additional providers. Do not upload private footage to obtain a reference without permission. Label illustrative work accurately; do not let a made-up asset become apparent evidence of client results.

## Check the chosen result

Inspect movement, object consistency, hands, text, logos, UI, cropping and resolution. Reject fabricated statistics or distracting artifacts. Confirm the chosen portion contains the needed action and enough usable handles. Do not compensate for an unsuitable source with increasingly elaborate effects.
