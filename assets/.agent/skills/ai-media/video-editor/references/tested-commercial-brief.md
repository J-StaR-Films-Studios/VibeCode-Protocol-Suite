# A tested commercial talking-head brief

This complete prompt was used for a fresh client-ad pass. The user liked the resulting edit. The reviewed run delivered actual sourced footage, varied visual treatments, retained the sales argument and made a focused creative correction to the rough. Inspection used chronological frames and metadata, not uninterrupted playback or listening. One successful case does not establish reliable performance on every brief.

The quoted prompt is a worked example, not current instructions or a fixed storyboard. It supplies the commercial job, creative latitude, permissions and completion boundary. Its asset permissions and music override apply only to that test.

## Complete tested prompt

```text
Turn the original A-roll in 01_Footage/A-Roll into a high-energy vertical client ad, 1080×1920 at 30 fps.

Use the video-editor skill. Work out the offer, audience and sales argument from the footage, then originate the creative treatment yourself.

I want a hyper-edited commercial with inventive motion, real imagery and purposeful B-roll. Show actions happening and illustrate relationships. Don’t repeat one card layout, split-screen or animation throughout.

You’re authorized to search online, download and use suitable free commercial-use B-roll, images and music. Verify licences and record sources. Actually use the assets, don’t just suggest them. Music is authorized for this pass, overriding any earlier silent-only brief. Keep the speech clear.

Preserve the service claims, team reassurance, amplification/scale benefit and booking CTA. Don’t invent results or portfolio evidence.

Treat this as a fresh edit, not a recreation of previous cuts. Author detailed briefs for motion passages, delegate where useful, review a moving rough cut, then deliver the finished MP4 and editable source.

Make creative decisions yourself. Ask before purchases, paid generation or private-media uploads.
```

## What to carry forward

The prompt makes the footage responsible for the offer and gives the agent responsibility for the treatment. It does not prescribe a shot for every line or ask the user to invent the motion. The agent can choose footage, imagery, typography, diagrams or A-roll according to the message.

"Hyper-edited" is paired with a commercial purpose and concrete visual jobs: show actions, explain relationships and preserve the offer. This gives ambition a direction without forcing a camera trick or effect onto every beat. Keeping the main argument protects the reason to hire, not merely the service list and CTA.

Explicit acquisition permission removes an avoidable ambiguity. "Actually use the assets" distinguishes a finished editing task from a list of recommendations. Detailed briefs are required for the selected motion passages, where exact choreography matters; the whole film is not forced into motion graphics.

The rough-cut review remains a creative responsibility. In the tested run, the parent corrected excess empty space and small photographic outputs in the amplification passage. Correct decoding or accurate pivots alone would not have identified that weakness.

## Adapt the brief, not the film

For a new assignment, determine the following from the user and actual material:

- The commercial job, supported offer, audience, required meaning and desired action.
- Delivery format and the requested degree of creative freedom or energy.
- Whether existing user work must be preserved, revised or deliberately ignored for a fresh pass.
- Asset acquisition, music and other finishing permissions. Do not inherit the example's authorization to download assets or override a silent brief.
- The actual deliverables and the boundary between a plan, rough and finished edit.

Write a compact assignment from those decisions. Keep routine art direction, visual selection and choreography with the agent when the user delegates them. Ask about consequential factual gaps or permissions, not for a supplied storyboard. An underspecified request is room for authorized creative judgment, not permission to fabricate business claims.

Preserve the current source's claims and argument instead of imposing team reassurance, amplification, scale or a booking CTA on every client. Do not automatically adopt vertical 30 fps, the tested runtime, its stock choices, palette, layouts or relationship diagram. High energy is a requested treatment, not the default for sermons or every talking-head video.

A coherent visual identity can span different treatments. Variety does not require unrelated styles, and a useful recurring device is not automatically a cliche. Inspect whether the chosen shots and motion serve their actual passages rather than enforcing a medium rotation or effects quota.
