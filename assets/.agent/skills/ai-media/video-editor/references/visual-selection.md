# Visual selection

## Start with the viewer's task

Ask what the viewer needs from this beat before choosing a medium. The following choices are starting points, not a mandatory rotation.

| Viewer need | Useful choices | Check before committing |
|---|---|---|
| Recognize something quickly | A short insert, familiar action or focused detail | Does it read during the actual few frames available? |
| Understand a concrete output | Finished work, product footage, screen recording, before/after when substantiated | Does it show the thing itself rather than a generic symbol or empty container? |
| Follow a process | Connected footage of successive actions, with selective labels | Do subject, screen content and shot order agree with the process? |
| Understand a relationship or progression | Diagram, motion graphic, comparison or mixed composition | Does movement explain the relationship, without implying unsupported quantities or results? |
| Trust the speaker or understand nuance | A-roll, deliberate framing and an occasional supporting reference | Would a cutaway hide expression or weaken the personal message? |
| Read an important phrase | Purposeful typography or a readable reference treatment | Does the audience have enough time to read it? |
| Know what to do next | Speaker and one clear action at a time | Is the requested action stronger than competing visual detail? |

Choose footage and graphics together when that improves clarity. A graphic can establish a category while footage demonstrates it. Avoid filling every region with simultaneous independent information.

## Select the actual shot

Reject a candidate that merely matches a keyword. Check:

- **Meaning.** Does the visible event support this exact passage? A handshake can communicate collaboration; it cannot demonstrate a creator recording a video.
- **Action.** What changes in the portion that will survive the edit? Avoid a long clip whose useful action occurs outside the selected range.
- **Readability.** Will the viewer recognize it at delivery size? Prefer clear subjects over clutter that needs explanation.
- **Continuity.** Do crop, lighting, direction of motion, subject and palette fit the surrounding shots? A script, product shoot and edit can follow the same subject when suitable assets exist. Do not claim continuity when the sources do not support it.
- **Information.** Does the shot add something beyond repeating the caption? Remove a redundant visual rather than decorate it further.
- **Truth.** Could the shot falsely imply a client relationship, real event, endorsement, measured result or theological claim?
- **Practical fit.** Is the source usable at the intended crop and resolution? Is its licence appropriate? Can its handles support the intended entrance and exit?

An attractive shot can still be the wrong shot. Do not silently settle for a weak asset. Recommend a specific alternative acquisition route or stay on A-roll while the needed material is unavailable.

## Design the time, not only the frame

Determine whether the beat needs a recognition flash, a readable hold or a developing explanation. Let this determine duration. A time metaphor may need a brief moving wristwatch shot, not a full countdown. If you choose a literal countdown, its values and progress must agree with elapsed time.

Show verbs happening. Specify what scrolls, reveals, moves, accumulates, compares or clears. A stationary stopwatch, motionless feed or unmoving progress indicator is not equivalent to the intended action.

Let graphics develop with the speech. A sentence may contain several distinct reveals, but do not force every word to animate. A hold is part of the animation. Clear a treatment when its job is finished rather than leaving stale labels over the next idea.

Use transitions for a reason. A push can move from an abstract statement into an example; returning to the speaker can reset attention. Do not add a transition effect to every cut. Avoid mechanical alternation between A-roll, stock, a zoom and a title.

Use source resolution for intentional reframing without stretching the face. Consider the speaker's eye line, gesture and looking space. The full layout may change over a passage; split-screen is an option, not a fixed frame for the whole video.

## Selective graphics

A graphic should emphasize, explain, orient or direct. Choose hierarchy and reading order before texture or decoration. Distinguish major statements from small contextual labels. Small labels should fit their text rather than occupy oversized panels.

Prefer a composed, readable treatment over a few words floating in a large empty board. Do not fill that board with unrelated objects to disguise the problem. Reduce its area or choose another composition.

Inspect real motion references when movement is the reference. Record the source and the decision worth adapting, such as reveal order, image integration, hold length or speaker framing. A still establishes appearance only. A template or library block is usable only if its layout and movement serve the current words.

Approval is local. If a staircase successfully explains growing output, retain it for that idea. It does not make staircases, illustrated cards, the same palette or the same headline treatment appropriate everywhere.

## Beat-plan format

Use a compact table when useful:

| Cue or range | Viewer need | Selected treatment and reason | Asset or acquisition route | Movement, hold and exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "More content" | See a variety of completed posts | Scrolling populated profile below the headline | Existing approved feed, otherwise source or generate illustrative content | Grid scrolls while the headline holds, then return to speaker |
| "Static designs" | See the deliverable | Finished image ad shown over the speaker | Approved portfolio sample or clearly illustrative licensed/generated artwork | Artwork enters, holds legibly, then clears |

Times must come from the current source or cut. Label proposals as proposed; do not invent precise speech synchronization from an approximate transcript. Keep this plan short enough to use while editing.
