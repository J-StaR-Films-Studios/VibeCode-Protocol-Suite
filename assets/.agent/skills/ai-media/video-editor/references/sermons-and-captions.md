# Sermons and requested captions

Load this reference for church or teaching edits, or for the caption preferences below. These instructions preserve useful guidance from the former Resolve skill. The current brief can override stylistic preferences, but cuts must not distort meaning.

## Preserve the teaching

Read the whole message before choosing a hook or removing repeated passages. Identify core claims, supporting scriptures, examples, qualifications and numbered points. A restatement may add a necessary distinction. Preserve negatives, caveats and context that prevent a claim from changing meaning.

Remove setup delays, dated announcements and redundant explanations only when expendable within the brief. A cold open must remain faithful to its original context. Do not shorten a sermon merely to hit a generic social-media duration. Keep emphasis, intentional pauses and breaths that make joins natural. Keep the preacher visible when expression or nuance carries the message.

Proof names, scripture references and uncertain speech by listening. Recognition may invent words in noise, silence or glossolalia. Do not manufacture captions or treat inferred language as a verified quotation.

## Support the passage visually

Use relevant references from sermon graphics, broadcast titles or editorial typography. Judge their actual movement when taking motion inspiration. A major statement may justify a composed treatment; passing references and chapter labels usually need less space.

Warm paper, sturdy serif type and restrained accent colour were useful in earlier sermon work. They are an optional starting point when appropriate, not the house style for every project. Integrate the speaker deliberately and protect face, gestures and microphone.

Feature main scriptures selectively. Verify wording and translation. Give an important passage a readable verse treatment with its reference and enough time to follow the reading. Split long passages at meaningful boundaries. Use a compact reference for passing mentions. Do not squeeze a whole verse into a corner label or cover the preacher with a large slab.

Stock and animation can support an exact passage when event footage is unavailable. Keep licence records and never imply stock footage depicts the service or event. Avoid literal imagery that changes the meaning of a theological claim.

## Captions remain optional

The user's previous sermon edit ultimately disabled burned-in captions. Leave them off by default unless the current brief requests them. This does not prohibit selective emphasis titles, service labels or scripture references. Preserve existing user captions unless authorized to change them.

For requested captions, preview a representative excerpt with speech before applying them throughout:

- Use short, complete, static phrases, usually two to five words, adjusted to the actual delivery.
- Split at audible pauses. Clear a phrase when it ends, especially across a meaningful gap.
- Do not expose a future clause through a pause or accumulate a long sentence.
- Do not progressively reveal words, move earlier words sideways, reflow a batch or use karaoke highlighting unless the user explicitly changes this preference.
- Recognition times and silence detection propose boundaries. Check quiet speech, audience noise and long pauses by listening.

Keep the caption layer separate and easy to disable. Respect rejection rather than making increasingly elaborate replacements. An SRT can be useful when requested; do not publish unproofed transcription.

## Packaging when requested

Use the relevant YouTube pipeline stage without changing its rules. Confirm church, channel and speaker names. Omit unknown attribution. Base titles and thumbnail copy on claims the message actually supports. Use actual speaker frames for image references; check identity and mobile readability, including duration-badge clearance.

Derive chapters from the latest edited timeline, beginning at video time zero. Recompute after further cuts or user changes. Check current platform requirements, dimensions, file sizes and chapter spacing. Prepare requested descriptions, tags, pinned comments and thumbnail options locally. Uploading or publishing requires separate authorization. Keep design judgment and technical checks distinct from audience testing.
