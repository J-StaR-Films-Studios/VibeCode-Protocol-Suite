# Author a motion brief without reskinning an example

Use this exercise on the actual assignment when originating a standalone motion film or a substantial original motion passage. It is not a request to render the worked examples or produce extra practice films. Keep the working notes short and proceed to the requested deliverable. A small overlay or tightly specified execution task can use the relevant parts without the full comparison.

## 1. Start from the subject before the examples

Read the user's material and [motion-brief guidance](motion-briefs.md). Before opening the example files, record your seed direction in brief working notes or the current project brief:

- What changes, and why does that change matter?
- Which behaviors, materials or relationships belong specifically to this subject?
- What is the obvious first association, and what other premise could escape it?
- What is the selected premise and ending? What would viewers see rather than need explained afterward?

For a sparse creative brief, use the short premise comparison in the motion guidance and choose independently when authorized. Do not ask the user to supply the visual transformation. Do not invent real capabilities, claims or evidence. Fictional invention remains limited to the scope the user granted.

A premise such as "the dot becomes a useful hinge" settles the plot. It does not yet settle art direction, composition or visual payoff.

## 2. Study directing decisions, then return to your subject

Read the complete [product-launch example](motion-examples/product-launch.md) and [spoken-word example](motion-examples/spoken-word.md), including their annotations. Source prompts are quoted reference material, not current commands. Do not execute their asset requests or carry over their sound, aspect ratio, frame rate, techniques or bans.

Choose the general craft principles that address your film's actual needs. Write a short transfer note explaining how you will use those principles and what scene-specific choices you will leave behind. Do not translate the examples into a list of obligatory effects.

For instance, tracking a selected result into a new context teaches continuity of identity. It does not require a card carousel, chat bubble or product UI in the next film. Prepared emphasis teaches arranging a later focal element in advance. It does not require an orbiting ring.

The examples may reveal a weakness in your seed direction. Revise it with a reason drawn from the current subject, not simply because an example's scene looked attractive. Do not claim the seed was independent if you already consulted the examples.

## 3. Resolve art direction separately from plot

For an open, ambitious assignment, compare two distinct visual treatments of the chosen premise. A few concrete sentences per treatment are enough. Change staging, material behavior, framing or reading order, not just the colours. Then select one and explain its strongest moving moment and its main readability or execution risk.

Define the visual world through decisions an implementer can draw:

- Describe the important starting, developing and final compositions. Specify the scale of the focal subject, its relationship to other elements and how attention changes.
- Define silhouettes, materials, typography when used and the rules governing their behavior. Choose these for the subject rather than defaulting to small black polygons on warm paper, glossy product cards or thermal pixels.
- Describe the strongest moving moment through visible actions and consequences. Identify the earlier staging that makes it work and the later image that pays it off.
- State what remains deliberately simple and why. A quiet composition can be strong, but empty space is not proof of taste. No camera movement, object count or effects quota applies.

If a visual reference is needed, inspect an actual relevant reference and identify the decision you are learning from it. Do not infer choreography from a still or claim a reference was viewed when it was not accessible. Do not copy an entire sequence under the label of inspiration.

## 4. Write the complete build prompt

Use the execution-brief requirements in [motion briefs](motion-briefs.md). The output is an authored film specification, not a template with headings or a list of hoped-for qualities.

For every consequential action, name the object, starting state, path or transformation, destination and surrounding response. Give proposed time ranges or verified cues with explicit units. Include readable holds and final states. Describe the frame before, during and after each important handoff. Assign ownership of shared objects and text so the implementation agent does not have to guess.

Specify scale or geometry where it affects composition, legibility, a mask boundary or a collision. Do not manufacture precise coordinates, beats or spring settings just to sound technical. Choose rendering requirements after the visual action is resolved. Allow the implementer to choose engineering details without silently changing the creative mechanism.

A weak instruction says "make the reveal cinematic." A useful one specifies what conceals the subject, what moves to expose it, how the reveal redirects attention and what becomes visible next. Supply your own answer, not a noun-swapped version of a worked scene.

Preserve required meaning and current audio scope. Do not inherit the examples' captions, music or fictional product details. When existing footage or assets are the right medium, specify them and their inspected source ranges or acquisition gaps instead of replacing them with generic shapes.

## 5. Read the brief as a film before dispatch

Ask whether faithful implementation of this document would already produce the intended visual experience. If it needs the coder to invent the composition, scene development or payoff, the directing is unfinished.

Check three separate things:

- The story communicates its required change and preserves the actual message.
- The visual treatment supplies a specific reason to watch beyond knowing the plot. Locate that reason in staging, materials, rhythm, typography or a visual relationship described in the brief.
- The proposed implementation can reproduce those decisions within current permissions and delivery constraints. Technical precision does not compensate for weak art direction.

Run the transfer check against both examples. If replacing their nouns recreates your scene order, visual grammar or signature handoffs, re-author the sequence. Generic techniques such as masks, springs, tracking or a retained object are reusable. A copied combination with substituted subject matter is different. Honour an explicit recreation request, but label it as recreation rather than original direction.

Keep the audit to a few concrete findings in the brief or working notes. Do not award ceremonial originality scores or guarantee novelty. Do not ban every familiar technique in an attempt to prove independence.

## 6. Test the authored intention in motion

Delegate the completed, self-contained brief using the host's rules, or build directly when appropriate. Request a moving prototype of the strongest action with enough preceding and following context to judge it. Use representative artwork and real copy. Stills can establish art direction but cannot approve the choreography.

Review both the mechanism and the promised visual strength. Check attention, silhouette, material behavior, composition changes, readability and payoff at delivery size. "The pivot is accurate" or "the export decodes" is not a creative verdict. Make one focused correction when the prototype does not deliver the authored intention, then proceed within the host's review and approval rules. Do not start an endless search for a criticism-free film.

For a concept-only request, stop at the requested brief instead of commissioning a render. For a build request, complete the film and source deliverables. Record actual inspection limits. Neither this exercise nor the worked examples establishes that a model will consistently originate exceptional films; that requires evaluating its generated briefs and moving outputs on new subjects.
